# PharmD sign-off packet: contraindicated and major rules (generated)

> Generated by `npx --no-install tsx scripts/signoff-packet.ts`. Do not hand-edit; re-run the script.

## Purpose

This packet lists every rule in the interaction engine that can produce a **contraindicated** or **major** finding, so that a pharmacist can approve, downgrade, or flag each one. Each row gives a plain description of what triggers the rule, how many catalog drug pairs it fires on, three example pairs, the source the app currently cites, and a blank reviewer column.

- **Status: no clinician has reviewed this content yet.** Every severity below is the current engine output, not a reviewed judgment.
- FirstPass is an educational interaction reference, not a substitute for clinical judgment, and not FDA-cleared.
- This packet contains no doses and makes no regulatory claims. Label references are paraphrases; open the Prescribing Information.

## How the counts were made

- Every unordered pair of the 1,918 catalog entries (1,838,403 pairs) was run through `analyze([a, b])` with no host context (no phenotypes, smoking, alcohol, renal, pregnancy) and no entered amounts. Host-, phenotype-, and dose-driven rules are therefore **not** counted here.
- "Pairs" = distinct drug pairs on which the rule produced a contraindicated/major finding. One pair can appear under several rules.
- PK rows are grouped by perpetrator strength × victim grade (the inputs to `pkSeverity()`), across all enzymes. A contraindicated row in that section is that grouping when the finding is tagged `label-contraindicated`; `pkSeverity()` does not emit that tier. The per-enzyme finding split is in the trigger column.
- "Current cited source" is what `basisFor()` in `src/lib/drugs/basis.ts` attaches today: a label/PI paraphrase keyed to the rule, or a drug-level PubMed cite, or nothing ("curated map only").

## 2. CYP / P-gp escalation (pkSeverity)

`pkSeverity()` never returns contraindicated. It stops at major (Serious concern) or lower. Avoid together on a CYP or P-gp row in this section is a `label-contraindicated` pin (`applyLabelPins`), not enzyme math. Inhibitors, including prodrug (activation) substrates: strong × sensitive or narrow-index → major; strong × major substrate → major; strong × otherwise → moderate; moderate × sensitive or narrow-index → major; moderate × major substrate → moderate; moderate × otherwise → minor; weak × sensitive or narrow-index → moderate; weak × otherwise → minor. Inducers on a clearance pathway: strong × any substrate, including minor → major; moderate × sensitive, narrow-index, or major → major; moderate × otherwise → moderate; weak → minor. Inducers on a prodrug (activation) pathway ignore sensitivity: strong → major; moderate → moderate; weak → minor.

## Known open questions

1. **CYP escalation to "contraindicated" is now label-only.** Since Ship 1.51 (7bdfa95), `pkSeverity()` never returns contraindicated. A strong inhibitor or strong inducer with a sensitive or narrow-therapeutic-index substrate stops at major ("Serious concern"), and every other branch stops at major or lower. "Avoid together" on a CYP or P-gp finding in this scan comes from a named label pin. On this scan **317 PK findings** (317 CYP, 0 P-gp) across **317 drug pairs** read contraindicated. All **317** of those findings are tagged `label-contraindicated` by `applyLabelPins()`. On **317** of those pairs no class/PD contraindicated rule also fires. Kaleb approved pinning the 91 label-quoted pairs that had fallen to Serious concern, each quoting its current DailyMed label. No severity is changed by this packet.
2. **Sodium oxybate (GHB) × non-hypnotic CNS depressants.** Rule `pd-ghb-cns` marks sodium oxybate with any CNS depressant, benzodiazepine, opioid, or alcohol as contraindicated (738 pairs). The oxybate labels contraindicate use with sedative hypnotics and alcohol; other CNS depressants are a warning. Of the 738 pairs, about 120 have an alcohol / benzodiazepine / Z-drug / hypnotic / barbiturate partner and about 618 have some other CNS depressant partner (heuristic split by PD flag and class name). Those others may belong at major.
3. **P-gp strength gaps.** FDA's reference table lists P-gp inhibitors without a potency grade, but the catalog stores a grade on every P-gp inhibitor role (currently 8 strong, 23 moderate, 8 weak), and `pkSeverity()` uses it exactly like a CYP grade. That drives 0 contraindicated and 500 major P-gp findings on this scan. Several drugs FDA lists as P-gp inhibitors (capmatinib, lapatinib, lopinavir/ritonavir, pirtobrutinib, propafenone, sofosbuvir/velpatasvir/voxilaprevir, tucatinib) have no P-gp inhibitor role here because a grade would have to be invented. A reviewer should decide how P-gp should be graded, or whether P-gp findings should be capped.
